Eye Punch

Hot hammer blank, 3 1/2 long by 1 3/4 wide by 2 tall. Put the eye dead center by eye, along the length and across the width. Turn the center punch dot on to practice or trust your eye. Drag the punch, strike, keep it cool, flip, find the dark spot, punch through.

Drag the punch over the blank. Center punch shows a dot dead center. Strike drives it. Dip cools it. Arrow keys nudge, P toggles the dot, space strikes, D dips.

How a real eye punch goes

Find the center first. Andrew scribes lines across the face with a carbide edge marker to find it, then sets one center punch dot where the middle of the eye goes. Some guys center punch both faces, front and back. One dot on the front is enough. The slot punch sits in that dot and does not walk. Turn the Center punch button on to see that dot. Turn it off to go by eye alone. Then drive it about halfway through. Pull it and dip it every two or three blows. Sticking is not the steel fighting the punch. It is the hot steel shrinking onto the cold punch so you cannot get it back out. Leave it in too long and the hole grabs it. Beat on it then and you wreck the eye and the tool. A pinch of coal dust in the hole is what lets it come back out clean. That is the punch lube smiths have always used. The dust burns off in the hole and the gas and carbon keep the hot steel from grabbing the cold punch. Flip the blank. The cold punch chilled a dark spot on the back. That is your mark. Set the punch on it and drive through to meet the first hole. The slug drops out. Then drift from both sides to open the eye.

Under the press it goes the same way. The long tapered punch hangs from the top die. A stripper plate with a slot through it sits on two threaded posts over the base, double nutted so the plate height is set to the blank. The blank goes in under the plate between the posts, end on. The plate holds the blank down so the punch pulls free of the hole when the ram comes back up. Without the plate the blank comes up with the punch. Without coal dust the hole grabs the punch and will not give it back. Pull anyway and the punch, the plate and the blank are all scrap. Lay it out cold first if you want. Scribe lines with the carbide marker and one center punch dot where the eye goes. The Center punch button puts that dot dead center so you can practice lining it up under the slot. Bring the ram down light. The tip drops into the dot and leaves a mark. Nudge and mark again if it is off. Pinch of coal dust in the mark. Drive most of the way through. Cool the punch. Flip, find the dark spot, mark, coal dust, drive, and the slug drops out.

This game is modeled on how Andrew at Portland Ironworks punches every hammer eye freehand, on the anvil and under his press. His eye is the fixture.
