    Why the spring matters

    Mechanical, Anyang 55 lb air, or press

    Mechanical hammer

    Motor, crank, coil spring and toggles, ram. Blows are tied to the crank so every stroke is the same height for a given treadle position. Old, cheap, loud and fast.

    Reach for it when
    • Drawing out long stock fast
    • Rough forging to shape
    • Repeat work where rhythm matters
    Watch out for
    • No single soft blow at speed
    • Spring and toggle tuning
    • Dies that hit cockeyed from worn guides

    Anyang 55 lb air

    A 5 hp motor drives a compressor piston. A valve routes that air to a second piston that carries the ram. Blow height, force and speed are all on the treadle, and the ram will sit up or clamp down on command.

    Reach for it when
    • One controlled hit, then a flurry
    • Holding the ram up or clamping work down
    • Tooling under the dies, spring swages, fullers
    Watch out for
    • Cost and weight
    • Valve timing and ring wear
    • Needs a foundation like any hammer

    Hydraulic press

    A pump pushes oil into a cylinder and a ram descends slowly with huge force. No blow at all, just a squeeze. Moves steel deep to the center instead of only at the surface.

    Reach for it when
    • Forge welding billets and cable
    • Punching, drifting and slitting with tooling
    • Precise sizing and bending at low heat
    Watch out for
    • Slow, so steel cools in the dies
    • Tooling must take full tonnage
    • Dwell time pulls heat into the dies
